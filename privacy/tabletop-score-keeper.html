Privacy Policy

Tabletop Score Keeper for Android

Peter Blasco

The short version

Tabletop Score Keeper does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. It has no analytics, no advertising, and no accounts. It does not request internet access, so it is not technically capable of sending anything anywhere.

What the app stores

The app saves the games you score: the names you type for players, their colours and turn order, the scores you enter, and — for Settlers of Catan — the dice rolls and robber events you log. It also remembers your setup preferences, such as the points target, and whatever you have left sitting in the tools.

All of this is written to the app's own private storage on your device. Other apps cannot read it, and the app itself never sends it anywhere — it holds no internet permission, so it could not if it wanted to.

There are exactly two ways that data moves off the device, and neither is the app sending it. You can export it yourself, and Android can back it up. Both are described below.

Player names are whatever you choose to type. They are labels on a scoreboard, and the app makes no attempt to connect them to a real identity. If you would rather not type real names, the app works exactly the same with nicknames or the defaults.

What the app does not do

  • No internet permission is requested, so no data can be transmitted.
  • No analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No accounts, sign-in, or cloud sync.
  • No access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone or photos.
  • No browsing of your files. Exporting and restoring a backup goes through Android's own document picker, which hands the app one file that you chose and nothing else.
  • Nothing is shared with third parties, because nothing is collected.

Exporting a backup

Settings can write every saved game to a single file, and read one back. You choose where that file goes through Android's document picker — your Google Drive, your Files app, a memory card, a message to yourself — and the app never learns where that is. It has no network permission, so it cannot put the file anywhere itself.

Once the file exists it is yours and it is outside the app. Whatever you have sent it to is governed by that service's terms rather than by this policy. The file is plain JSON, so you can open it in any text editor and see exactly what it contains.

Android's own backup

If backup is switched on for your device, Android copies this app's stored data to your Google account on its own schedule and restores it when you reinstall on a new phone. That is the operating system doing it rather than the app, it is governed by Google's terms and your device settings, and you can turn it off in your phone's own backup settings.

It is described here because "nothing leaves the device" would otherwise be inaccurate, and this policy would rather be exact than reassuring.

Installing from Google Play

The app collects nothing, but Google Play collects its own data about installs, updates, and crashes for every app distributed through it, including this one. That collection is Google's rather than this app's, is governed by Google's privacy policy and your own Play settings, and happens whether or not a developer asks for it. It is noted here so that "collects nothing" above is read as a statement about the app and not as a claim about Google.

Deleting your data

Deleting a game from within the app removes it permanently. Uninstalling the app removes everything it has stored. There is nothing held anywhere else, so there is nothing to request the deletion of and no one to ask.

If your device backs up app data to your Google account, that backup is governed by Google's terms and your own device settings rather than by this app. Any backup file you exported yourself is a file like any other, and deleting it is up to you and wherever you put it.

Children

The app is a scorepad for board games and is suitable for all ages. Because it collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children either.

Changes

If a future version of the app ever collects or transmits data, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here rather than made quietly.
